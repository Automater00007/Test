## 1. Project Overview

Jade River Resort is a luxury boutique resort in Pahalgam, Jammu & Kashmir, on the banks of the Lidder River. It is part of the Defoy Hotels & Resorts group.

The goal is to design and build a premium, modern, animation-rich website that positions Jade River as a distinctive, high-end destination rather than a generic hotel-booking template.

**Core brand line:** “Life, Slowed by the River.”

Every design and content decision should reinforce slowing down, immersion in nature, and boutique, personal attention. The resort has only 13 rooms.

### Goals

- Create a visually distinctive, animated, premium-feeling website rather than an outdated template-style hotel site.
- Build strong SEO, AEO (Answer Engine Optimization), and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) so the site performs well in traditional search and AI answer engines such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.
- Ensure the site is fully mobile-responsive, fast-loading, and accessible.
- Focus on direct bookings. The resort does not use OTAs such as MakeMyTrip or Booking.com; bookings happen directly through WhatsApp or contact.

## 8. Brand Voice & Design Direction

The site must not look like a generic, outdated hotel template. It should feel premium, cinematic, and distinctive — like a boutique lifestyle brand rather than a booking-engine skin.

### Brand voice

- Warm, unhurried, and specific; avoid generic “luxury” marketing filler.
- Reference concrete details such as wooden architecture, the stream, pine forest, and the sound of the river rather than vague adjectives.
- Let “Life, Slowed by the River” echo across the site, including the hero and philosophy section, without repeating it word for word everywhere. Vary the phrasing.

### Typography and color

Typography and colors are intentionally excluded from the final design direction for now. Use temporary, freely chosen typefaces and colors during prototyping so the layout, content, CMS, and interactions can be validated first. Treat these choices as placeholders and keep them centralized in theme tokens so the final visual system can be replaced without restructuring the site.

### Signature visual motif

A thin animated line styled like a flowing river or current should reappear across the site in different forms. Examples include a scroll-progress indicator in the hero, a divider in the Philosophy section, and a route line in the Location section. This should connect every section to the river and “slowed down” concept rather than making the site feel like disconnected sections.

## 9. Recommended Tech Stack

- **Framework:** Next.js 15+ with App Router, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS.
- **Animation:** Framer Motion for component-level animation and scroll-linked effects. Use CSS transitions and `IntersectionObserver` for simple fade/reveal animations because they are lighter and better for mobile performance than using a JavaScript library for everything.
- **Smooth scroll:** Lenis, disabled on mobile and when reduced motion is enabled for performance.
- **CMS:** Build the website as a CMS-first system rather than a static site. Use Sanity CMS for the blog/journal, rooms, experiences, gallery items, FAQs, moderated guest reviews, site settings, and other content the client should be able to update without a code deploy. Keep content models, validation, drafts, publishing, and preview workflows separate from presentation components.
- **Video:** YouTube Data API v3 to automatically pull videos from the resort’s YouTube playlist. Do not manually re-upload new videos.
- **Hosting:** Prefer Cloudflare for hosting and delivery, using Cloudflare Pages or Workers with the chosen framework’s supported deployment adapter. Use Cloudflare CDN, caching, image optimization, and edge delivery where appropriate. Confirm the final Pages/Workers setup and CMS webhook strategy before production.
- **Deployment path:** `defoyhotelsandresorts.com/jade-river-resort`, a sub-path of the parent group’s domain alongside properties such as Amber Spring and Twin Towers. Configure the framework and Cloudflare routing for the sub-path, and confirm the exact deployment approach with whoever manages the parent domain before finalizing it.

## 10. Key Features to Build

### A. Animation & Interaction

Every section should feel intentional rather than templated. The experience should include one memorable, “crazy” signature animation that makes a non-technical client immediately feel the quality of the site, while remaining lightweight and respectful of accessibility.

- Create a signature opening sequence: the river-line motif draws across the screen, the hero image or video appears through a fluid masked reveal, and the headline resolves in staggered layers before the page settles into the booking CTA. The effect should feel cinematic and surprising, not like a generic loading screen.
- Make the signature sequence interruptible and show it only once per session. Never block the first meaningful content or booking action behind the animation.
- Use GPU-friendly transforms and opacity, CSS masks or clip paths, compressed media, and small motion layers instead of expensive full-screen canvas effects or continuous CPU-heavy rendering.
- Use a cinematic video hero with staggered text reveal on load, loading a lightweight poster or low-resolution preview first and deferring heavier video.
- Add scroll-triggered reveal animations throughout with fade and slide effects while respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Add parallax imagery in at least one storytelling section, with a reduced or static fallback on mobile and reduced-motion devices.
- Add desktop-only 3D tilt-on-hover for room and blog-post cards; disable it on touch devices.
- Use a distinctive property-photo gallery treatment, such as a rotating 3D image ring or carousel, instead of a plain grid. Keep the interaction lazy-loaded and pause it when off-screen.
- Use smooth, physics-based scrolling site-wide only where it does not interfere with native scrolling, accessibility, or reduced-motion preferences.

#### Animation performance guardrails

- Animate `transform` and `opacity` wherever possible; avoid layout-triggering animations.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media and animation modules.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` with a clear static or minimal-motion version of every major effect.
- Disable or simplify parallax, 3D tilt, smooth scrolling, autoplay, and decorative loops on touch devices, low-power devices, and reduced-motion settings.
- Keep the first render, navigation, keyboard interaction, and direct booking CTA usable before decorative animation completes.
- Test the signature animation and page transitions on mid-range mobile hardware using Core Web Vitals, especially LCP, CLS, and INP.

- Automatically pull the latest videos from the resort’s YouTube playlist.

### F. Guest Reviews

Reviews are moderated.

- Guests submit reviews through a site form.
- Reviews are not published instantly. They are saved as drafts in Sanity and must be manually approved by an administrator before appearing publicly.
- The public form accepts only 3-, 4-, or 5-star ratings. This prevents unfair 1–2-star reviews, typically from non-guests or competitors.

## 11. SEO, AEO & GEO Requirements

This is a high priority. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) ensure that systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews can accurately find, understand, and cite information about the resort, in addition to supporting traditional Google search ranking.

### Required technical SEO elements

- Add structured data (JSON-LD):
  - `LodgingBusiness` site-wide with name, address, geo-coordinates, price range, and amenities.
  - `HotelRoom` on each room page.
  - `FAQPage` on the FAQ page.
  - `BreadcrumbList` on every inner page.
  - `AggregateRating` once real reviews exist.
- Create a dedicated, thorough FAQ page with clear, self-contained question-and-answer pairs. Cover location, pricing, booking policy, and experiences.
- Add an `llms.txt` file at the site root with a plain-text summary of the resort for AI crawlers.
- Configure `robots.txt` to explicitly allow AI crawlers (`GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`, and `CCBot`) alongside standard search engine bots.
- Generate a dynamic XML sitemap covering every static page and dynamic route, including rooms and blog posts.
- Add unique, accurate title and description metadata to every page; never duplicate metadata across pages.
- Add canonical URLs to every page.
- Use descriptive, specific image alt text everywhere; do not keyword-stuff it.

### Performance requirements

- Use a mobile-first workflow and test and optimize for mobile before desktop.
- Optimize for Core Web Vitals, including avoiding layout shift (CLS), minimizing main-thread blocking time (TBT), and keeping Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) fast.
- Compress and responsively serve all video and image assets. Never ship a multi-megabyte unoptimized video to mobile.
